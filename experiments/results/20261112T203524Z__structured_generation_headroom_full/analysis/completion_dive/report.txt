Completion failure analysis: November 12 MI355X full study

Scope and verified counts

The 528-attempt run contains 53 incomplete attempts across 20 distinct schemas
and 29 model/schema pairs. These are not 53 distinct schemas. There are 39 final
output exhaustions and 14 reasoning exhaustions. Every exhaustion consumed its
4,096-token stage budget. There are no runtime_failure records in this run.
Compiler rejections and completed schema-invalid outputs are separate outcomes.

                      Final incomplete    Reasoning incomplete
XGrammar                     23                     5
llguidance                   15                     4
Unconstrained                 1                     5

All 53 examined raw records match the sealed SHA256SUMS inventory. Analysis used
the original schemas, saved normalized backend schemas, original token IDs,
pinned tokenizer revisions and exact decoder versions: XGrammar 0.2.8 and
llguidance 1.9.1. All work was CPU-only on the Mac; no model was regenerated.

Observed mechanisms (mutually exclusive classification; total 53)

24 final whitespace loops
14 other constrained final loops/growth
14 reasoning-stage exhaustions
 1 unconstrained final output copying the schema instead of an instance

1. Whitespace loops: 24 attempts (15 XGrammar, nine llguidance)

The median whitespace suffix occupies 94.1% of the output's characters. A large
fraction of the 4,096 tokens therefore contributes no JSON structural progress.
Replaying the saved outputs using a byte vocabulary shows the exact endpoint:

13 are awaiting a colon after a property name;
 6 have comma as the only permitted non-whitespace next byte;
 4 permit comma and a closing bracket/brace;
 1 requires an opening object brace for a value.

Every endpoint allows at least one non-whitespace next byte. These are not
empty-mask failures. None permits EOS, because the document is unfinished.

A particularly important trigger is a mismatch between the model's preferred
property order and the backend's fixed property order. Among the 13 colon-wait
cases, the saved key resembles an intended key plus corrupt punctuation or
prose, e.g. radius**: 5.0**}**, displayName followed by a curly quote, or a long
servicePort key containing apparent comments. Those apparent comments are
actually inside a JSON property-name string; the parser has not escaped JSON.

For each case, replacing that corrupted key with its inferred intended key
makes the prefix fail in its original backend. In an XGrammar-only controlled
comparison, any_order=False rejects 12 of these 13 intended-key prefixes and
any_order=True accepts all 13. This isolates property ordering as a contributing
mechanism in 12 cases at the grammar level. It does not show that changing the
option would make the model complete successfully. The remaining description
case differs between backends and is not attributed to XGrammar ordering.

Minimal reproduction using the actual shape-control schema, control-04:

  {"shape":"circle","radius":5}

is valid under the original JSON Schema but rejected by both pinned decoders
with their tested property-order behavior. The equivalent object

  {"radius":5,"shape":"circle"}

is accepted. The schema lists radius before shape. Once shape is emitted,
closing the later radius key is blocked in this representation. Extra property
names remain possible because additionalProperties is allowed. The model emits
radius**: 5.0**}** as a single key, then thousands of newlines while the parser
waits for the real colon. Appending :null} completes the saved output, is accepted
through EOS by its backend, and passes the original-schema validator and the
study's duplicate-key-rejecting JSON parser.

Other whitespace cases reflect unfinished required structure. For example,
stress-20 has a Curve requiring at least four points; the Mistral-7B records
stall after three. A comma is allowed and closure is blocked. Whitespace lets
the model defer the required continuation indefinitely.

The tested XGrammar configuration left max_whitespace_cnt unset. Existing
whitespace limits are a relevant baseline, not a new research invention. A cap
can exclude the observed whitespace path; model reruns are needed to establish
whether it produces success or a different failure.

2. Other constrained final loops/growth: 14 attempts

Six repeatedly emit property names. Two stress-26 XGrammar attempts repeat
Twitter credential fields (315 and 336 duplicate occurrences in the scanned
prefix). Two stress-10 attempts repeat createOptions (76 duplicates each).
Two control-03 attempts repeat corrupted comment keys (570 duplicates each).
The grammar permits these streams, but the study parser rejects duplicate keys.
Those six saved prefixes cannot be made acceptable to this study merely by
appending a suffix; already-emitted duplicate names would need revision.
That is a serialization-contract mismatch, separate from the completion loop.

Three grow regex-constrained values because their expected closing quote is
not currently legal:

  stress-35, Qwen3-8B, both backends: the schemaCriterion resembles
  iglu:com.acme/user/1-0-0-1-0-0-...
  The regex requires vendor/name/format/version. The apparent version is still
  in the format segment: a slash and a version remain missing. CPU replay
  rejects an immediate closing quote, but accepts /1-0-0 followed by a quote.
  Thus the repeating hyphens are not required by the schema; they extend a
  segment that the model has failed to exit.

  stress-38, Mistral-Small-24B, llguidance: symbolExpression repeatedly uses
  %/s% instead of the required closing %s%. Replay rejects an immediate closing
  quote but accepts appending %s% followed by a quote.

The other five are two comment-inside-key loops (control-04), one escaped-key
loop (stress-30), one growing array after key corruption (stress-20 XGrammar),
and one increasingly nested structure after key corruption (stress-20
llguidance). The apparently free-form self-correction in the latter remains
embedded in JSON string/extra-property structures.

3. Reasoning exhaustions: 14 attempts, before final constrained decoding

Six are the thumbnail schema control-12 with Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B across all
three modes. The models generate long base64 image data while reasoning. A
saved, independently valid witness is only {"url":""}: the base64 expansion is
not required by an asserting schema constraint.

Three are Qwen3-8B stress-32 across all modes, constructing a long multi-year
course example inside reasoning. Two are Mistral-7B stress-39, enumerating the
large biosynthetic-cluster schema. Three are Mistral-7B stress-43, repeatedly
enumerating schema properties. The reasoning phase never reaches its required
boundary, so final decoding does not begin.

These 14 records reduce end-to-end success, but are not evidence of a failure
of the final-output constraint engine. Repeated runs of the same model/schema
across modes reproduce the same saved reasoning token stream when available.

4. Remaining unconstrained final exhaustion: one attempt

Mistral-7B stress-40 outputs the schema's $defs and property definitions in a
Markdown JSON block instead of producing an instance. It is still copying the
large schema when the final budget is exhausted.

What the replays establish

For all 38 constrained final exhaustions, replaying every original model token
with its pinned tokenizer succeeds; decoded text matches the saved output.
At the saved endpoint, the next-token mask is nonempty, the matcher is not in
an accepting state, and EOS is disallowed. The byte-vocabulary replay agrees
that each prefix is accepted. No parser/accept failure is observed.

For 15 saved prefixes, a simple append-only suffix was constructed, validated
against the original schema using the study's strict JSON parser, and accepted
through EOS by the corresponding grammar. These are constructive examples of
available success that the model did not choose. No model regeneration was
performed; these suffixes do not change original outcomes.

All 20 affected schemas have an independently rechecked successful output
elsewhere in this same run, with the shortest observed successful final output
between 16 and 869 tokens. The schema tasks themselves therefore do not
intrinsically require more than 4,096 final tokens. This does not establish
that every failed prefix remains short-completable or that a particular model
will find that output without a changed decoding policy.

Research interpretation and next discriminating experiment

The evidence supports a completion/progress problem caused by model/grammar
interaction, not a claim that these tasks are inherently too long. It does not
demonstrate an empty-mask dead end. It also does not prove every accepted
prefix is extendable to an original-schema-valid result: six prefixes already
violate the study's duplicate-key policy, and constructive completions were
only established for 15 of 38 saved prefixes.

The most informative next experiment holds schemas, model revisions, seeds,
hardware, reasoning prompts and budgets fixed and varies one factor at a time:
first the existing whitespace bound; then XGrammar's existing any_order option;
then an explicit progress/completion policy. Reasoning failures require their
own comparison, since final-output masks cannot affect a stage they never reach.
Do not claim these existing configuration options as novel gap resolutions.

No rerun or billed compute was launched in this analysis. No per-step logits
were saved, so alternative model probabilities and the counterfactual effect
of different masks cannot be reconstructed. CPU acceptance changes establish
which paths are allowed; they do not measure model success after interventions.

Follow-up: predicted termination behavior from the saved histories

46 attempts show strong evidence of persistent repetition: the 24 whitespace
loops; 13 of the other 14 constrained loops/growth cases (excluding the growing
numeric array); six base64 reasoning loops; and three repetitive schema-advice
reasoning cases. This is a qualitative "unlikely to finish soon without a
change" assessment, not a proof of infinite generation or a measured probability.

Seven remain indeterminate: three course-plan reasoning drafts, two exhaustive
finite schema walks, one finite schema-copying output, and one growing numeric
array. The three course-plan drafts are the best candidates for ordinary budget
truncation because they are drafting the third planned year. None can honestly
be classified as proven to succeed after just a few additional tokens. In
particular, finishing reasoning or finishing copying a schema is not the same
as returning a schema-valid instance.

The analysis examines the full saved generation history, not just the final
characters. Long repeated tails and lack of structural progress support the
loop classification. Replay establishes which next steps are legal. A manually
constructed short completion proves that success is available, not that the
model would choose it. Even for greedy decoding, repeating visible output does
not prove that the full model state repeats: its context continues to grow.
Actual future behavior requires model continuation experiments from the saved
prefixes under the original decoding settings. Those experiments were not run.
